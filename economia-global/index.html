Reading a REER index: a rising line means the currency has strengthened in real (inflation-adjusted) terms against its trading partners; a falling line means real depreciation — the right gauge of competitiveness and external-adjustment pressure, unlike the nominal rate which mixes the two together  |  Try it: pick a region to load all its economies at once, or tick individual countries; switch the time range to zoom into a single episode (e.g. 2008–09 or 2020–22); and turn on a moving-average overlay to separate the underlying trend from short-run noise — each overlay is computed over the full 1994–2026 history so it stays valid even when zoomed in  |  Starting comparison (default selection): the United States against five large Latin American economies — Argentina, Brazil, Chile, Colombia and Peru — whose currencies show far larger real swings than the dollar's, the empirical signature of the asymmetry between a reserve-currency issuer and the emerging markets that absorb the swings in capital flows it attracts and repels (the Triffin/Mehrling theme running through this dashboard)  |  Source: BIS Effective Exchange Rate statistics (WS_EER)
